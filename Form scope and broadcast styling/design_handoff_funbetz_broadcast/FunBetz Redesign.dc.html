Turn 1 · broadcast scoreboard direction

Two close variants of the pick → ticket → league loop

Same foundation in both: cool-graphite base, Barlow Condensed for anything that behaves like a scoreboard, IBM Plex Mono for numerals, a live lock countdown pinned under the wordmark, and a week that opens on what is bettable now. They differ in the pick surface and in what the shareable artifact is. Both are live — pick 3+ games, build the ticket, then hit Settle week on the Leagues tab to watch legs resolve. Reference 1a or 1b in chat.

1a Scoreboard

Games stack vertically as away-over-home rows, the way a broadcast scoreboard reads. Each row is a 46px target with an LED indicator, so a pick is one tap and never a mis-tap. Lime is the pick colour, amber is money, and a live ticker carries results and other people's hits.

1b Stub

Keeps the side-by-side matchup you have today, but the pick slabs lead with the team abbreviation at scoreboard scale. The countdown becomes one large clock with a window-progress rule, and the ticket is a cream paper stub with punched notches — the thing worth screenshotting.

Ports cleanly

Both variants keep your component anatomy intact: GameRow, locked variant, week header, floating ticket bar, ticket, status pill, league card. The selected state is still a border + tint + colour swap, so it stays one conditional class string in TSX. Team badges are still 26px circles with an initial-letter fallback.
